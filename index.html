About color vision deficiency

The human eye has three types of cone cells, most sensitive to long (L, red), medium (M, green) and short (S, blue) wavelengths. When one type is missing or shifted, some colors collapse into each other. Prevalence figures are approximate and vary by population.

Deuteranopia / deuteranomaly

≈ 6% of men · most common

M cones are missing or shifted. Reds, greens, oranges and browns look alike.

Protanopia / protanomaly

≈ 2% of men

L cones are missing or shifted. Red/green confusion, and reds look much darker, close to black.

Tritanopia / tritanomaly

≈ 1 in 10,000 or rarer · all sexes

S cones are missing or shifted. Blue and green look alike, and yellow can look pink or light gray.

Achromatopsia

≈ 1 in 30,000

No working cones. Only lightness is seen, often with sensitivity to bright light.

Do

  • Pair every color with a second cueUse an icon, a text label, a pattern or a shape (WCAG 1.4.1).
  • Underline links in body textOtherwise they need 3:1 contrast with the text around them, plus a non-color cue on hover and focus.
  • Meet contrast minimums4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for icons, borders and chart lines.
  • Label charts directlyName lines at their ends and use distinct markers and dash patterns.
  • Test with several simulationsA palette that is safe for one type can fail another.

Don't

  • Show success and failure by red vs. green aloneThat pair fails for about 8% of men.
  • Code meaning in blue vs. green or yellow vs. pinkTritan viewers confuse these pairs.
  • Mark errors with a red border onlyAdd an icon and a message that says how to fix it.
  • Show focus or selection by hue aloneUse outlines, weight, fills or shape changes.

How the simulation works. A color matrix is applied in linear RGB to the specimen. Protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia use the full-severity matrices from Machado, Oliveira & Fernandes (2009). Lower severities blend linearly toward normal vision, which approximates anomalous trichromacy. Achromatopsia maps each color to its relative luminance. Simulations are a guide and won't match any one person's vision exactly.
